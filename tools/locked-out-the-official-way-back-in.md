# Locked Out — The Official Way Back In, for Every Account

Forgotten password, changed phone number, or a hacker who got there first — and
then the worst discovery: there is no one to call. This is the real way back in,
in plain English. Every step below is free, and every one of them happens inside
the companies' own pages — nowhere else.

From ztrader88 — plain-English safety lessons. Stay safe online: no hype, no
fear-selling.

## First, the two rules that protect you

1. **There is no support phone number.** Facebook, Instagram and Google do not
   offer a customer-service phone number for ordinary users. Any "support
   number" you find in an ad, a comment, or a search result is a scam.
2. **Recovery is always free.** Anyone offering to unlock your account for a
   fee is running a scam. The real way back costs nothing.

## Facebook — two doors, pick the right one

- **You forgot your password, or lost access to your email or phone:**
  start at **facebook.com/login/identify** — the "find your account" door.
  If the email address or phone number it offers you is dead, use the
  **"No longer have access to these?"** link to route around it.
- **Someone got into your account and changed things:** use
  **facebook.com/hacked** — the compromised-account door. It is built for
  exactly the case where the contact details on the account are now the
  attacker's, not yours.
- Facebook may ask you to prove it is really you with a government ID or a
  video selfie. That is normal, and it happens inside Facebook's own flow.
- **Suspended or disabled account?** Appeal windows exist, and they expire —
  don't sit on it. Follow the appeal path Facebook shows you when you try to
  log in.
- **Ignore any guide that tells you to use "Trusted Contacts."** Facebook
  discontinued Trusted Contacts in 2022. Old guides still recommend it; it no
  longer exists.

## Instagram

- Start at **instagram.com/hacked** — the official recovery hub. Its choices
  cover a hacked account, a forgotten password, and losing access to your
  email or phone. The flow sends you a login link or a security code, and the
  identity check can be a video selfie.
- **The email that can save you:** if someone changes the email address on
  your Instagram account, Instagram emails your *previous* address from
  **security@mail.instagram.com** with a way to reverse the change. If you get
  one of those and it wasn't you, act on it straight away.

## Google

Google publishes its own recovery rules. The short version:

- Use a device, browser and place you usually sign in from — Google grades
  your attempt partly on whether it looks like you.
- Answer as many questions as you can. Skipping questions hurts.
- If it asks for the last password you remember, enter the **most recent**
  one you can recall — not the oldest.
- Enter an email address that is connected to the account, and check your
  spam folder for Google's reply.
- **Google never asks for your password or sign-in codes by email, phone or
  message.** Only ever type them at accounts.google.com.

## "Can I just talk to a human?"

The honest answer: mostly, no — and anyone promising you a human for a fee is
selling the scam above. What does exist:

- Meta launched a support hub inside the Facebook and Instagram apps
  (December 2025). Meta says it raised hacked-account recovery success by
  more than 30% in the US and Canada during 2025 — that's Meta's own figure.
  The catch: the hub lives *inside* the apps, so if you are fully locked out,
  start with the web doors above.
- Meta Verified — Meta's paid subscription — includes chat and email support
  that can help with hacked accounts. The catch people report: that support
  sits inside the account you are locked out of. Prices vary, so check Meta's
  own page rather than anyone's summary.

## Why this matters more than it used to

In June 2026, news broke that one of Meta's own AI-assisted rescue tools had
been turned against users: between April 17 and May 31, 2026, up to 20,225
Instagram accounts were taken over through it, before Meta shut the tool down
and cancelled its reset links. One detail from that incident is worth
remembering: **accounts with two-factor authentication turned on held.**

## Before it happens to you

Turn on **two-factor authentication** (2FA) — the setting that asks for a
code from your phone as well as your password when you sign in. It takes a
few minutes, and it is the lock that held when the rescue tool itself was
turned. Do it on your email account first: email is the master key to
everything else, because every "forgot password" reset lands there.
