## What this is

This is the handover Day 2 of the series teaches: one small job, given
plainly, with its finish line stated and the never list checked first. It is
blank on purpose — the shape is the lesson, and the job will be yours.

A first job is not a test the agent can fail in some grand way. It is a
handshake. You are not trying to impress it, and it is not trying to impress
you. You are finding out how it works, while the stakes are a sorted list.

## How to use it

1. **Pick the job first.** The piece names three that fit: listing what is
   in your inbox and who each message is from; sorting the pile of post on
   the counter by who it is for; or pulling the week's appointments into one
   clean list. Any job like them works. The test that matters: small enough
   to check in ten seconds, and you would know at a glance whether it is
   right. Pick the dullest one. Dull is a compliment here — dull means you
   already know exactly what right looks like.
2. **Copy the block** between the two lines marked COPY FROM HERE / COPY TO
   HERE, further down this page.
3. **Fill in the [square brackets] in your own words**, then give the brief
   to your agent — paste it into the conversation, or read it aloud. It
   stands behind your instructions page from Day 1; the page says who it
   works for and what it never does, and this brief says today's job.
4. **Run the never-list check before you send the brief.** Four boxes. If
   any box won't tick honestly, the job is not a first job yet — pick a
   smaller one.
5. **After the run, fill in the last part while it is fresh**: the one true
   fact you learned about your agent by watching it work. That line is not
   paperwork — it is the first line your agent's notebook will ever hold
   (Day 3's piece), learned today, while it is still true and still in your
   head.

## What each part does

- **The job, in one plain sentence** — said the way you would say it to a
  person standing in the room. If the sentence needs a second sentence to
  explain it, the job is too big for a first job.
- **What finished looks like** — the list, the order, the names: what you
  will actually see when the job is done. This is your finish line from
  Day 1, applied to one job. Judge the work against it, kindly and plainly
  — that is what it is for.
- **The never-list check** — the four things a first job must not do:
  sends nothing, spends nothing, deletes nothing, publishes nothing. A job
  that passes all four can be watched calmly, and whatever it teaches you
  is cheap at the price.
- **What I noticed** — one true fact about your agent, learned by watching:
  where it was careful, where it guessed, where it asked. One fact, honestly
  come by, is worth more than a week of wondering what it might be like.

## The job, in one plain sentence

[Say it the way you would say it to a person standing in the room.]

## What finished looks like

[What you will see when it is done — the list, the order, the names —

## The never-list check

This job:

- [ ] sends nothing
- [ ] spends nothing
- [ ] deletes nothing
- [ ] publishes nothing

(If any box won't tick, it isn't a first job yet.)

## What I noticed (fill in after the run)

[One true fact about your agent, learned by watching it work.]
